# Feature Selection and Variable Selection

## Selection objectives

- **Predictive selection** - Chooses a small or inexpensive input subset for a specified learner and prediction task. Its risk-cost trade-off need not identify a unique set of generating variables. [Guyon and Elisseeff 2003](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v3/guyon03a.html> "An Introduction to Variable and Feature Selection; JMLR").
- **Minimal-optimal versus all-relevant** - A small subset sufficient for prediction and the full set of relevant variables are different targets; redundant variables can matter to the latter. [Nilsson et al. 2007](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v8/nilsson07a.html> "Consistent Feature Selection for Pattern Recognition in Polynomial Time; JMLR").
- **Conditional discovery** - Model-X knockoffs test whether the target is independent of a variable given all remaining inputs. This target differs from keeping every useful substitute. [Candes et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02351> "Panning for Gold: Model-X Knockoffs for High-dimensional Controlled Variable Selection; JRSS B").
- **Unsupervised selection** - Usefulness is defined by a chosen surrogate, such as reconstruction, neighborhoods or cluster structure. Preserving it does not establish utility for an unspecified target. [Li et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.07996> "Feature Selection: A Data Perspective; ACM CSUR").
- **Acquisition** - Static selection uses one subset; an adaptive policy chooses the next measurement from values already observed. This differs from selecting an explanation after seeing all inputs. [Covert et al. 2023](<https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/covert23a.html> "Learning to Maximize Mutual Information for Dynamic Feature Selection; ICML").

### Interactions and interchangeable variables

- **XOR example** - For independent fair bits, Y = X1 XOR X2 is independent of either input alone, yet determined by their pair. Even nonlinear univariate dependence scores can miss joint information. [Brown et al. 2012](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v13/brown12a.html> "Conditional Likelihood Maximisation: A Unifying Framework for Information Theoretic Feature Selection; Journal of Machine Learning Research").
- **Duplicate example** - If X1 = X2 = Y, either input predicts perfectly, and each is conditionally uninformative given the other. Changing between these subsets need not change predictions. [Statnikov et al. 2013](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v14/statnikov13a.html> "Algorithms for Discovery of Multiple Markov Boundaries; JMLR").

## Comparative evidence

- **Tabular feature-selection benchmark** - On 12 datasets with MLP/FT-Transformer predictors, Deep Lasso improves prediction with corrupted or second-order added features. Products of original variables can be useful; rejecting every added feature is not the objective. [Cherepanova et al. 2023](<https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05877v1> "A Performance-Driven Benchmark for Feature Selection in Tabular Deep Learning; NeurIPS").
- **SelectArena** - Across 71 datasets and five learners, tree importance and F-tests offer strong prediction/runtime trade-offs. Selection uses fixed subset sizes and a one-hour cap, with random filling/subsampling for interrupted searches; validity measures rejection of added noise. [Schäfer et al. 2026](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13644v2> "SelectArena: A Modern Benchmark for Feature Selection on Real-world Tabular Data; arXiv").

## Filter criteria and screening

### Marginal screening

- **Variance threshold** - Removes features with low training-set variance without using labels; scale affects the result. [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/feature_selection.html#removing-features-with-low-variance>).
- **Univariate tests** - Chi-squared for non-negative counts/frequencies; ANOVA F for class differences; linear-regression F for marginal linear association. Individual scores can miss interactions. [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_selection.chi2.html>), [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_selection.f_classif.html>), [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_selection.f_regression.html>).
- **Sure independence screening (SIS/ISIS)** - Screens marginal correlations before subset fitting; ISIS repeats screening. Signal/design assumptions give high-probability retention of all signals, while extra variables may remain. [Fan and Lv 2008](<https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0612857> "Sure Independence Screening for Ultra-High Dimensional Feature Space; JRSS B").

### Information, redundancy, and interactions

- **Relief** - Updates feature weights from nearby same-class and different-class examples. Relief-family review covers interaction sensitivity and extensions. [Kira and Rendell 1992](<https://www.aaai.org/Papers/AAAI/1992/AAAI92-020.pdf> "The Feature Selection Problem: Traditional Methods and a New Algorithm; AAAI"), [Urbanowicz et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08421> "Relief-Based Feature Selection: Introduction and Review; Journal of Biomedical Informatics").
- **MI estimation** - Discrete and continuous features require appropriate treatment; mislabeling their type can give incorrect estimates. Nonlinear dependence estimation does not make marginal scoring interaction-aware. [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_selection.mutual_info_classif.html>).
- **Joint mutual information (JMI)** - Scores candidates jointly with already selected variables. Brown et al. derive JMI and related criteria as approximations to conditional-likelihood selection. [Yang and Moody 1999](<https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/1999/hash/8c01a75941549a705cf7275e41b21f0d-Abstract.html> "Data Visualization and Feature Selection: New Algorithms for Nongaussian Data; NeurIPS"), [Brown et al. 2012](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v13/brown12a.html> "Conditional Likelihood Maximisation: A Unifying Framework for Information Theoretic Feature Selection; Journal of Machine Learning Research").
- **FCBF (fast correlation-based filter)** - Uses symmetrical uncertainty to screen relevance and remove redundant features. [Yu and Liu 2003](<https://www.aaai.org/Papers/ICML/2003/ICML03-111.pdf> "Feature Selection for High-Dimensional Data: A Fast Correlation-Based Filter Solution; ICML").
- **Consistency-based selection** - Penalizes identical selected-feature patterns with conflicting labels. Dash and Liu compare search strategies using this criterion. [Dash and Liu 2003](<https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(03)00079-1> "Consistency-based search in feature selection; Artificial Intelligence").
- **Mutual information** - A dependence quantity; Kraskov et al. provide an estimator. Selection criteria include mRMR/JMI, while forward search constructs a subset. These are separate choices. [Kraskov et al. 2004](<https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0305641> "Estimating Mutual Information; Physical Review E"), [Brown et al. 2012](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v13/brown12a.html> "Conditional Likelihood Maximisation: A Unifying Framework for Information Theoretic Feature Selection; Journal of Machine Learning Research").
- **mRMR** - Balances feature-target mutual information against redundancy among selected features. [Hanchuan Peng et al. 2005](<https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2005.159> "Feature selection based on mutual information criteria of max-dependency, max-relevance, and min-redundancy; TPAMI").
- **INTERACT** - Removes features using changes in class inconsistency, targeting interactions that marginal ranking can miss. [Zhao and Liu 2007](<https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/07/Papers/187.pdf> "Searching for Interacting Features; IJCAI").

## Conditional independence and Markov boundaries

- **Koller-Sahami** - Eliminates features using approximate Markov blankets and information-theoretic redundancy criteria. [Koller and Sahami 1996](<https://ai.stanford.edu/~koller/Papers/Koller+Sahami:ICML96.pdf> "Toward Optimal Feature Selection; ICML").
- **Grow-Shrink** - Grows a candidate blanket and then removes conditionally irrelevant variables. [Margaritis and Thrun 1999](<https://robots.stanford.edu/papers/Margaritis99a.pdf> "Bayesian Network Induction via Local Neighborhoods; NeurIPS").
- **IAMB** - Incremental association-based blanket discovery, with forward inclusion and backward pruning. [Tsamardinos et al. 2003](<https://aaai.org/papers/flairs-2003-073/> "Algorithms for Large Scale Markov Blanket Discovery; FLAIRS").
- **Max-Min blanket discovery** - Identifies parents/children and extends the search to a target's Markov blanket. A predictive blanket alone does not identify causal direction. [Tsamardinos et al. 2003](<https://doi.org/10.1145/956750.956838> "Time and sample efficient discovery of Markov blankets and direct causal relations; KDD").
- **Markov blankets** - Seek variables that render the target conditionally independent of the remaining variables. Discovery depends on conditional-independence tests and distributional assumptions. [Fu and Desmarais 2010](<https://www.iaeng.org/publication/WCE2010/WCE2010_pp321-328.pdf> "Markov Blanket based Feature Selection: A Review of Past Decade; WCE").
- **Markov boundary** - An inclusion-minimal blanket. Multiple boundaries can exist, yielding alternative sufficient subsets; disagreement need not identify a uniquely wrong selection. [Statnikov et al. 2013](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v14/statnikov13a.html> "Algorithms for Discovery of Multiple Markov Boundaries; JMLR").
- **FOCI** - Greedily adds variables using a conditional-dependence statistic. Its sufficient-set recovery result needs detectable improvement at every insufficient subset; purely joint signals can defeat this forward-search condition. [Azadkia and Chatterjee 2021](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12327> "A simple measure of conditional dependence; The Annals of Statistics").

## Subset search and cardinality optimization

- **Floating search** - Alternates inclusion and exclusion to revisit earlier choices. Adaptive floating search varies the search depth. [Pudil et al. 1994](<https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-8655(94)90127-9> "Floating search methods in feature selection; Pattern Recognit. Lett."), [Somol et al. 1999](<https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-8655(99)00083-5> "Adaptive floating search methods in feature selection; Pattern Recognition Letters").
- **Sequential selection** - Greedily adds or removes features according to cross-validation scores. SequentialFeatureSelector supports forward and backward search. [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_selection.SequentialFeatureSelector.html>).
- **Population-based subset search** - Genetic search and particle swarms explore subsets scored by a chosen criterion; that criterion defines the selection objective. [Miao and Niu 2016](<https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2016.07.111> "A Survey on Feature Selection; Procedia Computer Science"), [Kennedy and Eberhart 1995](<https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/150GA/homeworks/hw3/_reading6%201995%20particle%20swarming.pdf> "Particle swarm optimization; ICNN"), [Tu et al. 2007](<https://www.iaeng.org/IJCS/issues_v33/issue_1/IJCS_33_1_18.pdf> "Feature Selection using PSO-SVM; IAENG IJCS").
- **Wrappers** - Evaluate candidate subsets using a particular learner's predictive performance. Search and validation are part of the method. [Kohavi and John 1997](<https://machine-learning.martinsewell.com/feature-selection/KohaviJohn1997.pdf> "Wrappers for feature subset selection; Artificial Intelligence").
- **Recursive feature elimination (RFE)** - Repeatedly fits a model and removes low-ranked inputs. RFE and RFECV implement elimination and cross-validated subset-size selection. [Guyon et al. 2002](<https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1012487302797> "Gene Selection for Cancer Classification using Support Vector Machines; Mach. Learn."); [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_selection.RFE.html>), [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_selection.RFECV.html>).
- **Best-subset optimization** - Mixed-integer formulations constrain feature count directly. Solver bounds can certify global optimality or quantify a remaining gap when stopped early. [Bertsimas et al. 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.03133> "Best Subset Selection via a Modern Optimization Lens; The Annals of Statistics").
- **Fast L0 selection** - Coordinate descent and local combinatorial search optimize L0-penalized objectives. Their local optimality conditions differ from a global MIO certificate. [Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01454> "Fast Best Subset Selection: Coordinate Descent and Local Combinatorial Optimization Algorithms; Operations Research").

## Embedded sparsity and model-based selection

### Penalized and Bayesian models

- **Spike-and-slab** - Mixture priors represent exclusion and inclusion of regression coefficients. George and McCulloch develop computational approaches. [Mitchell and Beauchamp 1988](<https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1988.10478694> "Bayesian Variable Selection in Linear Regression; JASA"), [George and McCulloch 1997](<https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Approaches-for-bayesian-variable-selection.pdf> "Approaches for Bayesian Variable Selection; Statistica Sinica").
- **Lasso** - An L1 penalty yields sparse regression coefficients, coupling variable selection with fitting. [Tibshirani 1996](<https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1996.tb02080.x> "Regression Shrinkage and Selection Via the Lasso; JRSS B").
    - **Adaptive lasso** - Weights each coefficient's L1 penalty using an initial estimate. Zou establishes selection consistency and oracle asymptotics under design, initialization and tuning conditions; this is not a finite-sample guarantee of correct support. [Zou 2006](<https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000000735> "The Adaptive Lasso and Its Oracle Properties; JASA").
    - **Lasso support recovery** - Sparse prediction does not establish correct variable recovery. Selection consistency requires additional assumptions, notably restrictions on predictor dependence. [Zhao and Yu 2006](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v7/zhao06a.html> "On Model Selection Consistency of Lasso; JMLR").
- **Elastic net** - Combines L1 and L2 penalties, encouraging groups of correlated predictors to enter together. [Zou and Hastie 2005](<https://web.stanford.edu/~hastie/Papers/B67.2%20(2005)%20301-320%20Zou%20&%20Hastie.pdf> "Regularization and variable selection via the elastic net; JRSS B").
- **Group lasso** - Selects predefined groups of coefficients, such as all encoded levels of a categorical variable. This differs from elastic net's grouping of correlated predictors. [Yuan and Lin 2005](<https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2005.00532.x> "Model Selection and Estimation in Regression with Grouped Variables; JRSS B").
- **HSIC Lasso** - Uses feature-wise kernels to select variables with strong nonlinear dependence on the response and limited redundancy with one another; a sparse alternative to linear Lasso screening. [Yamada et al. 2014](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.0515> "High-Dimensional Feature Selection by Feature-Wise Kernelized Lasso; Neural Comput.").

### Trees, importance, and subset rules

- **Decision trees** - Split-based importance sums impurity reductions attributed to each input; thresholding can select a subset. These scores can favor high-cardinality inputs. [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.tree.DecisionTreeClassifier.html>), [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_selection.SelectFromModel.html>).
- **Gradient boosting** - Sequential function fitting can supply feature-importance rankings. Importance thresholding produces a subset. [Friedman 2001](<https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1013203451> "Greedy function approximation: A gradient boosting machine.; Ann. Statist."), [Mason et al. 1999](<https://papers.nips.cc/paper/1766-boosting-algorithms-as-gradient-descent.pdf> "Boosting Algorithms as Gradient Descent; NeurIPS"); [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_selection.SelectFromModel.html>).
- **Random forests** - Ensembles provide importance scores used for ranking and selection. Boulesteix et al. review practical pitfalls. A ranking still needs a subset-selection rule. [Breiman 2001](<https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1010933404324> "Random Forests; Mach. Learn."), [Boulesteix et al. 2012](<https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/13766/1/TR.pdf> "Overview of random forest methodology and practical guidance with emphasis on computational biology and bioinformatics; WIREs Data Min &amp; Knowl").
- **Boruta** - Targets all-relevant selection by comparing random-forest importances with randomized shadow features. [Kursa et al. 2010](<https://www.mimuw.edu.pl/~ajank/publications/kursa-boruta-2010/Kursa2010.pdf> "Boruta – A System for Feature Selection; Fundamenta Informaticae"), [Kursa and Rudnicki 2010](<https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v036i11> "Feature Selection with the Boruta Package; J. Stat. Soft."); [code](<https://github.com/scikit-learn-contrib/boruta_py>).
- **VSURF** - Forest importance and stepwise selection produce subsets for interpretation and prediction. [Genuer et al. 2010](<https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00755489/file/PRLv4.pdf> "Variable selection using Random Forests; Pattern Recognition Letters"), [Genuer et al. 2015](<https://doi.org/10.32614/RJ-2015-018> "VSURF: An R Package for Variable Selection Using Random Forests; The R Journal").

### Differentiable selectors

- **Concrete autoencoders** - Learn which original inputs to retain through a relaxed discrete selector and reconstruction decoder. [Balın et al. 2019](<https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/balin19a.html> "Concrete Autoencoders: Differentiable Feature Selection and Reconstruction; ICML"); [code](<https://github.com/mfbalin/Concrete-Autoencoders>).
- **Stochastic gates (STG)** - Learn input gates jointly with a predictor using a continuous relaxation of an L0 feature-count penalty. [Yamada et al. 2020](<https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/yamada20a.html> "Feature Selection using Stochastic Gates; ICML"); [code](<https://github.com/runopti/stg>).
- **LassoNet** - Ties nonlinear feature use to sparse linear skip connections through a hierarchical constraint; produces a regularization path of global subsets. [Lemhadri et al. 2021](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-848.html> "LassoNet: A Neural Network with Feature Sparsity; JMLR"); [code](<https://github.com/lasso-net/lassonet>).
- **Deep Lasso** - Applies a group penalty to loss gradients with respect to input features, extending linear sparsity to input sensitivity. Gradient norms also rank features. [Cherepanova et al. 2023](<https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05877v1> "A Performance-Driven Benchmark for Feature Selection in Tabular Deep Learning; NeurIPS").

## Unsupervised and graph-based selection

- **FSSEM** - Searches feature subsets alongside expectation-maximization clustering; examines likelihood and scatter criteria and their dimensionality biases. [Dy and Brodley 2004](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v5/dy04a.html> "Feature Selection for Unsupervised Learning; JMLR").
- **Laplacian Score** - Ranks features by preservation of local graph structure; applicable without class labels. [He et al. 2005](<https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2909-laplacian-score-for-feature-selection.pdf> "Laplacian Score for Feature Selection; NeurIPS").
- **Principal feature analysis** - Uses PCA-derived structure to choose representative original variables. Its output is an input subset rather than principal-component coordinates. [Lu et al. 2007](<https://doi.org/10.1145/1291233.1291297> "Feature selection using principal feature analysis; ACM Multimedia").
- **Spectral feature selection (SPEC)** - Evaluates features through a similarity graph's spectrum, covering supervised and unsupervised criteria. [Zhao and Liu 2007](<https://doi.org/10.1145/1273496.1273641> "Spectral feature selection for supervised and unsupervised learning; ICML").
- **Multi-cluster feature selection (MCFS)** - Combines a spectral embedding with sparse regression to select features preserving cluster structure. [Cai et al. 2010](<https://doi.org/10.1145/1835804.1835848> "Unsupervised feature selection for multi-cluster data; KDD").

## Resampling, error control and post-selection inference

- **Ensemble selection** - Aggregates feature preferences across perturbed datasets or selectors. Aggregation alone supplies no generic FDR guarantee. [Saeys et al. 2008](<https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/535025> "Robust Feature Selection Using Ensemble Feature Selection Techniques; ECML PKDD"), [Shen et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05003> "A Novel Weighted Combination Method for Feature Selection using Fuzzy Sets; FUZZ-IEEE").
- **Stability selection** - Thresholds selection frequencies across subsamples. Its original bound controls the expected number of false selections under exchangeability and other assumptions. [Meinshausen and Buehlmann 2010](<https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.2932> "Stability Selection; JRSS B").
    - **Complementary-pairs stability selection** - Pairs disjoint half-samples and bounds selection of variables with low selection probabilities, without the original exchangeability requirement. [Shah and Samworth 2012](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.5578> "Variable selection with error control: Another look at Stability Selection; JRSS B").
- **Artificial controls, different targets** - Boruta compares forest importances with shuffled shadows for all-relevant selection; model-X uses exchangeable controls for conditional discovery with FDR control under its assumptions. [Kursa and Rudnicki 2010](<https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v036i11> "Feature Selection with the Boruta Package; J. Stat. Soft."), [Candes et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02351> "Panning for Gold: Model-X Knockoffs for High-dimensional Controlled Variable Selection; JRSS B").
- **Knockoff+** - Selects against synthetic control variables with finite-sample false discovery rate control in the paper's Gaussian linear-model setting. [Barber and Candès 2015](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5609> "Controlling the false discovery rate via knockoffs; Ann. Statist.").
- **Post-selection inference** - Conditions on lasso selection to form intervals for selected-model coefficients. Exact results use a fixed design and Gaussian errors with known variance. [Lee et al. 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6238> "Exact post-selection inference, with application to the lasso; Ann. Statist.").
- **Model-X knockoffs** - Permits arbitrary response models; FDR control requires exchangeability and knockoffs independent of the response conditional on the inputs. [Candes et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02351> "Panning for Gold: Model-X Knockoffs for High-dimensional Controlled Variable Selection; JRSS B"); [docs](<https://web.stanford.edu/group/candes/knockoffs/>).
    - **Deep knockoffs** - Learns approximate model-X knockoff generators by encouraging pairwise exchangeability. Generator diagnostics matter: approximate sampling alone does not establish exact false-discovery control. [Romano et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.06687> "Deep Knockoffs; JASA").
    - **Knockoff robustness** - Flexibility in the response model relies on modeling the input distribution. Errors in estimated feature conditionals can inflate FDR; robustness bounds quantify this dependence. [Barber et al. 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.03896> "Robust inference with knockoffs; The Annals of Statistics").

## Instancewise selection

- **Instancewise selection** - L2X learns a feature subset for each example to explain a fixed predictor; this differs from one global subset used for all observations. [Chen et al. 2018](<https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/chen18j.html> "Learning to Explain: An Information-Theoretic Perspective on Model Interpretation; ICML").
- **INVASE** - A selector, predictor and baseline network learn instance-specific feature subsets through actor-critic training; the selected variables and subset size can change between observations. [Yoon et al. 2019](<https://openreview.net/forum?id=BJg_roAcK7> "INVASE: Instance-wise Variable Selection using Neural Networks; ICLR").

## Cost-sensitive and adaptive acquisition

- **Dynamic feature selection** - Learns a policy approximating greedy conditional-mutual-information acquisition from observed inputs. Recovery of the greedy policy assumes optimal training; it is not a globally optimal acquisition guarantee. [Covert et al. 2023](<https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/covert23a.html> "Learning to Maximize Mutual Information for Dynamic Feature Selection; ICML").

## Evaluation and comparative evidence

### Validation and selection stability

- **Selection leakage** - Choosing features before resampling can bias estimated prediction error. Ambroise and McLachlan demonstrate the problem in microarray classification; pipeline guidance keeps selection inside training folds. [Ambroise and McLachlan 2002](<https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.102102699> "Selection bias in gene extraction on the basis of microarray gene-expression data; PNAS"); [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html#data-leakage>).
- **Stability measurement** - Compares scores, ranks, or subsets under data perturbations. Nogueira, Sechidis, Brown provide a chance-corrected measure, intervals, and tests. [Kalousis et al. 2006](<https://cui.unige.ch/~kalousis/papers/stability/KalousisPradosHilarioKIS2007.pdf> "Stability of feature selection algorithms: a study on high-dimensional spaces; Knowl Inf Syst"), [Nogueira et al. 2018](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/17-514.html> "On the Stability of Feature Selection Algorithms; Journal of Machine Learning Research").
- **Search overfitting** - A noisy validation criterion can overfit selector, subset-size and predictor choices. Nested resampling or an untouched test set must evaluate the complete search, including learned preprocessing. [Cawley and Talbot 2010](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html> "On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias in Performance Evaluation; JMLR").
- **MUVR** - Combines variable selection with repeated double cross-validation in multivariate modeling. [Shi et al. 2018](<https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/35/6/972/5085367> "Variable selection and validation in multivariate modelling; Bioinformatics"); [code](<https://gitlab.com/CarlBrunius/MUVR>).

### Correlated features and importance bias

- **Forest importance bias** - Strobl et al. show preference for variables with more categories or different measurement scales in studied forest importance measures. Importance is not an unbiased relevance test. [Strobl et al. 2007](<https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-8-25> "Bias in random forest variable importance measures: Illustrations, sources and a solution; BMC Bioinformatics").
- **Conditional permutation importance** - Permutes within a conditioning scheme to account for predictor dependence. Gregorutti et al. analyze correlation effects and recursive elimination. [Strobl et al. 2008](<https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/2821/1/deck.pdf> "Conditional variable importance for random forests; BMC Bioinformatics"), [Gregorutti et al. 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5726> "Correlation and variable importance in random forests; Statistics and Computing").
- **Held-out permutation importance** - Measures a fitted model's score loss after permuting a feature. Correlated substitutes can mask importance; the result depends on the model and score. [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/permutation_importance.html>).

## Task-specific selection and method recommendation

- **Selector recommendation** - Learns from dataset metafeatures and previous selector evaluations. [Wang et al. 2013](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0570> "A Feature Subset Selection Algorithm Automatic Recommendation Method; Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research"), [Parmezan et al. 2017](<https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2017.01.013> "Metalearning for choosing feature selection algorithms in data mining: Proposal of a new framework; Expert Systems with Applications"), [Shen et al. 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09856> "A Novel Meta Learning Framework for Feature Selection using Data Synthesis and Fuzzy Similarity; FUZZ-IEEE").
- **Uplift modeling** - Selects inputs for heterogeneous treatment-effect prediction rather than ordinary outcome prediction. Zhao et al. develop task-specific statistical and information-theoretic criteria. [Zhao et al. 2022](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03447> "Feature Selection Methods for Uplift Modeling and Heterogeneous Treatment Effect; IFIP AICT").

## Software

- **Python (classical selection)** - scikit-learn provides filters, model thresholds, RFE/RFECV, and sequential selection; scikit-feature collects research implementations. [docs](<https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/feature_selection.html>), [code](<https://github.com/jundongl/scikit-feature>).
- **Python (learned selection and error control)** - Concrete autoencoders, STG, LassoNet, knockpy. [code](<https://github.com/mfbalin/Concrete-Autoencoders>), [code](<https://github.com/runopti/stg>), [code](<https://github.com/lasso-net/lassonet>), [docs](<https://amspector100.github.io/knockpy/>).
- **Supporting tools** - feature-selector removes missingness/constant/collinearity-based columns; these are data-quality rules. BoostARoota implements XGBoost-based shadow comparisons. [code](<https://github.com/WillKoehrsen/feature-selector>), [code](<https://github.com/chasedehan/BoostARoota>).
- **R (search and filters)** - FSinR and praznik; copent supplies supporting copula/transfer-entropy estimation. [Aragón-Royón et al. 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10330> "FSinR: an exhaustive package for feature selection; arXiv"), [Ma 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14025> "copent: Estimating Copula Entropy and Transfer Entropy in R; arXiv"); [code](<https://cran.r-project.org/package=FSinR>), [code](<https://cran.r-project.org/package=praznik>), [code](<https://cran.r-project.org/package=copent>).
- **R (model-based selection)** - Boruta, VSURF, glmnet, spikeSlabGAM, knockoff. [Scheipl 2011](<https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v043i14> "spikeSlabGAM: Bayesian Variable Selection, Model Choice and Regularization for Generalized Additive Mixed Models in R; J. Stat. Soft."); [code](<https://cran.r-project.org/package=Boruta>), [code](<https://cran.r-project.org/package=VSURF>), [code](<https://cran.r-project.org/package=glmnet>), [code](<https://cran.r-project.org/package=spikeSlabGAM>), [code](<https://cran.r-project.org/package=knockoff>).
- **L0Learn** - Approximate L0-regularized regression and classification through coordinate descent and local combinatorial search; C++ implementation with R/Python interfaces. [Hazimeh et al. 2023](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v24/22-0189.html> "L0Learn: A Scalable Package for Sparse Learning using L0 Regularization; JMLR").
- **Archived software** - fscaret was archived by CRAN in August 2025; retained for historical reference. [code](<https://cran.r-project.org/package=fscaret>).

## Surveys and further reading

- **An Introduction to Variable and Feature Selection** - Relevance, redundancy, ranking, subset search, and validation. [Guyon and Elisseeff 2003](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v3/guyon03a.html> "An Introduction to Variable and Feature Selection; JMLR").
- **Feature-selection survey** - Reviews filter, wrapper and embedded approaches. [Miao and Niu 2016](<https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2016.07.111> "A Survey on Feature Selection; Procedia Computer Science").
- **Feature Selection: A Data Perspective** - Conventional, structured, heterogeneous, and streaming data. [Li et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.07996> "Feature Selection: A Data Perspective; ACM CSUR").
- **Feature Selection and Feature Extraction in Pattern Analysis** - Selection of existing variables and construction of new representations. [Ghojogh et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02845> "Feature Selection and Feature Extraction in Pattern Analysis: A Literature Review; arXiv").
- **Attribution rankings** - Man-Chan compare permutation, LIME and SHAP rankings for random forests; reported stability differences do not establish predictive gains from selecting those features. [Man and Chan 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12483> "The best way to select features?; The Journal of Financial Data Science").
